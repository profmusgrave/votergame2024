How scoring works

Each voter is worth up to 1,000 points. Say 100 percent and be right and you get all 1,000; say 100 percent and be wrong and you get nothing. Saying 50 percent always earns 750. In between, points are 1,000 × (1 − error²), where error is the gap between your probability and what happened. This is the Brier score turned upside down, so hedging is safe but knowing something pays.

After each answer you see two benchmarks: the share of Trump voters among other survey respondents who match what you were told, and the prediction of a logistic regression on the full set of demographics.

Calibration

Your guesses grouped into bins. Points on the diagonal are well calibrated; above the line means those voters went for Trump more often than you said.
